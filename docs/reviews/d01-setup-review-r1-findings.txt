R1 - Medium: separately issued board CLI credential has no callable lifecycle
Location: setup.txt:87-90,208-214,309-326,337-339.
Accepted basis: 05:18-25,34-44; 04:101-109; 18:30-46.

- Native owner renewal is defined, but expressly does not regenerate board CLI
  credentials. The journey proceeds from grant.create to a browser session code.
  It never identifies how a CLI receives its separately issued 30-day credential,
  or reauthenticates after ordinary expiry, credential-file loss or revocation.
- Accepted 05 distinguishes a durable grant from a credential and says
  reenrollment must not upgrade that grant. Accepted 18 makes grant.create a
  ledgered mutation with immutable identity. Replaying that mutation to recover
  a lost bearer is not a credential-reissue contract and cannot be inferred.
- Consequence: normal CLI use and original-actor outcome lookup after 30 days
  need a consequential implementation choice, despite the proposed freeze.

Bounded closure criteria:
- Define the issuance/reissue handoff for a PREEXISTING enabled grant, naming
  the callable CLI/API or a separately owner-accepted contract dependency and
  the implementation gate it controls. Keep protocol work with its proper
  owner; no general installer or workspace scope is required.
- State who authorizes issuance, exact installation/board/incarnation/actor/
  grant binding, private token delivery/storage, expiry, current-right checks,
  restart persistence and revocation. Specify whether reissue revokes previous
  credentials and how lost/uncertain delivery is resolved without replaying
  grant creation. Expired/lost bearer possession cannot itself confer authority.
- Keep grant receipts/results as durable non-secret IDs; exclude bearer secrets
  from fingerprints, operation outcomes, exports and recovery evidence. A grant
  may be confirmed while delivery of its credential remains failed or unknown.
- Add synthetic first issue, expired/lost token reissue, revoked grant refusal,
  interrupted delivery and same-actor original-outcome lookup vectors. Issuance
  must not change actor, broaden rights or rewrite immutable submitted evidence.

R2 - Medium: native owner lacks defined lookup authority for its grant actions
Location: setup.txt:199-205,309-315,337-339.
Accepted basis: 03:51-58,68-75; 04:101-109; 05:18-32; 18:30-46.

- The native owner has grant-admin only and no board grant. Accepted 03/04
  requires current read-own-outcome authority to return terminal operation
  evidence; accepted 18 includes grant.create/revoke in that ledger.
- After the first grant.create response is lost, a new human grant belongs to
  the selected grantee, not necessarily the native owner's ownerActorId.
  Issuing that grantee a credential does not authorize the owner's original
  operation lookup. The contract supplies neither an applicable current right
  nor an explicit bounded denial/recovery decision for that native principal.
- Consequence: the bootstrap journey cannot promise reconciliation of its own
  lost administrative response. Inferring grant-admin as general board read or
  silently giving the owner read-own-outcome would change accepted authority.

Bounded closure criteria:
- Resolve the authenticated native-owner lookup policy for its own grant
  operations, with the appropriate accepted clarification. State exact board/
  incarnation/actor/opId/fingerprint binding and current authority after owner
  renewal; do not implicitly grant board read/write, approve or history-admin.
- Alternatively identify an explicit accepted recovery-stop and gated decision,
  including its availability cost. Never claim the missing grant response is
  NOT_COMMITTED, infer success from a visible grant, or replay grant.create.
- Add first-grant lost response, owner expiry/renewal before lookup, wrong actor/
  fingerprint and denied-current-authority vectors. Lookups remain read-only;
  missing/inaccessible evidence remains unknown. Coordinate this seam with R1.

Assessed boundaries without further material finding
- Grammar, canonical public identities, fixed native paths and versioned local
  generation formats are concrete. Port 47443, key lifetimes and publication
  protocol remain proposals for Terry, as setup.txt:11-17 states. Owner direction
  supports one Go module and web/ package in the public implementation repository.
- Exclusive dual-loopback binding, exact name/SNI/Host/Origin, local issuer pin,
  manual trust enrollment/removal and qualified browser evidence are separated.
  A successful init is neither port reservation nor browser trust proof.
- Leaf expiry has a callable stopped/exclusive renewal and startup renewal path
  (179-186). Native owner expiry has a callable same-actor native renewal,
  independent of its expired bearer (208-214). These ordinary paths are not
  deferred to a generic repair decision. Status distinguishes expiry from damage
  (379-391), and no hot renewal or perpetual running availability is promised.
- Owner-file/key corruption or loss is fenced rather than identity-regenerated
  (208-214,269-276). Incomplete/ambiguous setup transactions preserve evidence and
  stop for a scoped repair decision (253-267). This intentionally sacrifices
  availability; it is not successful automatic recovery. Exact complete pending
  candidates can be reconciled without minting another identity or bearer.
- Issuer near-expiry, expiry, lost key or compromise deliberately stops the
  installation for a separately accepted replacement/migration decision
  (188-197). This can prevent original-authority lookup indefinitely; 341-345
  preserves that uncertainty. Public-only trust removal remains callable when
  secret material is unavailable (288-305). No root rotation, global revocation,
  secure erasure or automatic adoption of old evidence is claimed.
- Atomic generation selection, exclusion, retained retired credentials and
  possible-effect outcome rules preserve coherent authority and honest unknowns.
  The native primitives and durability qualification remain explicit future
  evidence, rather than being certified by this documentary review.
- Native Windows/amd64/local NTFS, private-at-creation ACLs, exact trust-store
  membership, browser/profile behavior and fault evidence remain prerequisites.
  Same-account cooperative assurance remains explicit; terminal confirmation is
  neither hostile isolation nor proof that a human meaningfully reviewed work.
